We find new homes for the clothes fashion brands can't sell.

Thread Futures is a B2B resale service for fashion brands: we act as their sales team for stock they can't sell themselves (checking, finding buyers such as off-price retailers/resale platforms, contracts), and give them the money from each sale plus a record of which approved buyer took each lot, and what they declare happened next.

We start with premium brands, for what their sample sale and archive didn't sell: new-with-tags stock, sold in small lots to trade buyers the brand approves by name. The brand keeps ownership of the stock until it is sold, buyers pay the brand directly, and the brand sees its net £ on every lot before it commits.

We make the better option the easy one, then use what we learn to help brands make less.

In brief

The service, the buyer and the route in.

What Thread Futures does, who pays, how we reach them, and why a service rather than software. Beliefs below are hypotheses to test, not findings.

Go-to-market

Brands are the buyer.

Brands are the buyer. We reach them directly and through their warehouses (3PLs); the brand's own 3PL does the physical work on every batch, and we test 3PL introductions in parallel.

How a batch works

The brand sends a stock list (no system access). Its jobber gives a written bid: that is the floor. We split the stock into small unbranded lots; the brand's own warehouse (3PL) does a carton check and one photo per style, on the brand's instructions. Trade buyers the brand approves by name send written bids. If the bids beat the floor by enough, the brand sells; otherwise there is no batch and nothing to pay.

The brand keeps ownership of the stock until it is sold, invoices each buyer and is paid directly. We invoice only our fee. Transport and warehouse work are arranged with and paid by the brand, at cost.

What we take on

Average RRP £120 or more (£80–120 only if the floor bid is 6% or less); new with tags or as-new; no more than two seasons old, or continuity lines; a batch of at least £50,000 at RRP (usually 300+ items; hard floor 200 units), and a brand's leftovers can be combined across seasons. Lots are curated, 20–200 units each, with a pass-through target of £1.50 a unit or less.

Not for us: lower-price ranges, returns or damaged goods sold for cash, and pallet-size mixed lots.

Brand protection

Your name is kept off lot sheets and resale listings; the labels the law requires stay on. You approve every buyer by name, buyers agree not to use your brand name on resale sites or actively market into your markets, and we never show your floor to buyers or other brands.

We won't offer lots to your existing stockists, and buyers agree not to actively market into territories you have given exclusively to a distributor.

Why a service, not software

Each batch is run by hand. Until the 26 Feb 2027 decision we build nothing new: we use spreadsheets (the Google Sheet CRM, lot sheets, bid form and calculator), and scripts only when a real stock list or a real bid run needs them. No hosted app, no logins, no Stock Health index.

In the EU, large brands can no longer destroy unsold clothes and shoes they hold there, except on narrow documented grounds (since 19 July 2026; medium-sized firms from July 2030). Great Britain has no equivalent ban: for stock held in Great Britain, the case is money and discretion. The legal routes are narrow and need paperwork: sell, donate, or prove an exception before recycling. Up to €100,000 in Germany. Records for any destruction under an exception must be kept 5 years. Business model · fee rule v4

How we earn: one fee per batch, only on the extra.

Our fee is the larger of £1,500 or 30% of the uplift, where the uplift is buyer money minus pass-through costs minus the brand's written jobber bid. The minimum applies from the first batch, there is no cap, and the minimum batch is £50,000 at RRP. A batch goes ahead only if the uplift is at least £1,500; otherwise the brand keeps its jobber bid and pays nothing. In the brand's words: "Our fee comes only out of the extra above your jobber's written bid: the larger of £1,500 or 30% of the extra. If the extra doesn't cover it, the batch doesn't go ahead, and you pay nothing."

Take rate: On a £54k batch at 10–13% bids our fee is about 21–28% of buyer money, more than B-Stock (~10%) or Liquidity Services (~11%), because you pay only on the money above your own written offer, and nothing if we don't beat it. Partial sales: On the bids, you never end up below your written jobber bid. Every bid records a valid-until date. Insolvency lots: proposed: the larger of £2,500 or 12% of realisations, still to be decided; we act as agent of the office-holders.

Price evidence, with its caveats: trade prices seen are ~25% of RRP for recognised brands vs ~10% for little-known premium labels (IQR 7–13%); insolvency stock ~5–7%. The test on Sun 13 Dec 2026 decides: 3 written prices on recognised brands and 3 on independent premium; bids decide.

Moat

Why brands and buyers won't go around us.

Today, none. Non-circumvention terms are a weak moat. The only defensible assets we can build are a clearing-price dataset from written bids and a pool of buyers signed to destination terms; both need transactions. We will report two moat numbers in every update: bids logged and buyers signed to terms.

Decision

What would change our mind

We stop or pivot if any of these happens:

  • 3 or more of the first 5 premium-lot prices come in below the floor + 3 percentage points, or the median is below 10% of RRP.
