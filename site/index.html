We find new homes for the clothes fashion brands can't sell.

Thread Futures is a B2B resale service for fashion brands: we act as their sales team for stock they can't sell themselves (checking, finding buyers such as off-price retailers/resale platforms, contracts), and give them the money from each sale plus a record of which approved buyer took each lot, and what they declare happened next.

We start with premium brands, for what their sample sale and archive didn't sell. The brand keeps ownership of the stock until it is sold, buyers pay the brand directly, and you see your net £ on every lot before you commit.

How it works

Your sales team for stock you can't sell.

Five steps per batch, run by hand. You decide at the end, with the numbers for every lot in front of you.

  1. 01

    Send a list

    You send a stock list. No system access needed.

  2. 02

    Your floor

    Your jobber gives a written bid: that's your floor. No jobber? We agree a written floor with you before we start.

  3. 03

    Small lots

    We split the stock into small unbranded lots. Your own warehouse (3PL) does a carton check and one photo per style, on your instructions.

  4. 04

    Buyers bid

    Trade buyers you approve by name send written bids.

  5. 05

    Sell, or stop

    If the bids beat your floor by enough, you sell: buyers pay you, your 3PL releases the goods, and you get a record of which buyer took each lot. Otherwise, no batch and nothing to pay.

Last resortAnything no buyer takes can go to a charity or recycler the brand contracts directly, with the evidence logged. Transport and warehouse work are arranged with and paid by the brand, at cost.

What brands get

More back. On your terms.

We only go ahead if trade buyers beat your current written offer, after warehouse costs and our fee. You see the numbers per lot before you decide.

Money back

On the bids, you never end up below your written jobber bid. Small, curated lots go to several trade buyers, so each lot can go to its best bid.

Your name kept off

Your name is kept off lot sheets and resale listings; the labels the law requires stay on. You approve every buyer by name, buyers agree not to use your brand name on resale sites or actively market into your markets, and we never show your floor to buyers or other brands.

Your stockists respected

We won't offer lots to your existing stockists, and buyers agree not to actively market into territories you have given exclusively to a distributor.

Records you can use

Records in the categories your disclosure and exception files use. Compliance stays with you.

The rules

If you hold stock in the EU.

In the EU, large brands can no longer destroy unsold clothes and shoes they hold there, except on narrow documented grounds (since 19 July 2026; medium-sized firms from July 2030). Great Britain has no equivalent ban: for stock held in Great Britain, the case is money and discretion.

Why I care

Over the last five years it feels like we've been buying more than ever, and what bothers me most is how normal it's become. Perfectly good clothes get shredded or burned because that's cheaper than finding them a home. I don't think the answer is making people feel guilty. I think it's making the better option the easy one: finding good clothes a new home, and being able to show where they went.

FAQ

Good questions.

Something else? Send us a message.

Do you buy the stock?

No. The brand keeps ownership of the stock until it is sold.

Do you take the money?

No. The brand keeps ownership of the stock until it is sold, invoices each buyer and is paid directly. We invoice only our fee. Any warehouse work is approved by you in advance and invoiced to you by your warehouse at cost.

Who is it for?

Premium fashion brands (average RRP £120+), usually with no outlet, for new-with-tags stock their sample sale and archive didn't sell. Larger groups with stock held in the EU have extra reasons, set out above.

Why not just use a jobber or liquidator?

You may well keep using one. We ask for your jobber's written bid first and treat it as your floor. Your 3PL does a carton check and one photo per style; buyers bid on that, in small lots, and you approve every buyer by name.

If the bids don't beat your floor by enough, the batch doesn't go ahead: you take your jobber's bid and pay us nothing.

How is this different from other services?

Several good services exist: jobbers, B2B marketplaces such as Unfrosen, and funded platforms such as Ghost and smatch. We are for brands that want every buyer approved by name and want to see, before they commit, what they net on each lot against their own written jobber bid.

Is the brand liable if a buyer destroys the goods?

This isn't settled yet. Every buyer agrees to terms on what happens to the goods next and signs a statement of where each lot went, and we plan to have a lawyer review those terms. This is general information, not legal advice.

Why not just use ChatGPT?

A chatbot can explain the rules or analyse a stock file. It can't approve buyers, get written bids from trade buyers on a real lot, or check goods physically. The record is made of documents created at each handover. We do use AI where it saves time, such as reading stock lists and drafting records, and a person reviews anything you sign off.
